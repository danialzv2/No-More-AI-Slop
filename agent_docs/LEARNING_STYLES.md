# Study Notes and Slides

Use this guide with the shared writing rules in [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md). Follow the assignment brief or supplied template for required content, length, and layout.

## Teach the topic

- Organize the material around what the reader needs to understand or do. Introduce each term before using it in a diagram, calculation, or example.
- Explain a difficult idea with a worked example. For calculations, show the starting values, meaningful steps, units, and what the result means.
- Use a flowchart for a process with branches, a table for a comparison, or a chart for a trend when it makes the idea easier to understand. Give visuals clear labels and include units, legends, or sources where needed.
- Choose visuals that explain the material or show a real example. Skip decorative images, repeated icons, and illustrations that add no information.
- Remove stock introductions, repeated takeaways, and vague claims. Include a short practice question or revision prompt where it helps the learner check their understanding.

## Study notes

- Use headings that name the topic. Keep explanations short enough to scan and detailed enough to study independently.
- Keep definitions, examples, and supporting visuals near the relevant explanation. Use bullets for genuinely parallel points and numbered lists for steps.
- Highlight terms, formulas, or common mistakes selectively. Keep the surrounding explanation readable without relying on highlighting.

## Slides

### Theme and typography

- Use white (`#FFFFFF`) as the slide background, deep navy (`#00203E`) for titles and body text, and gold (`#FFBF00`) as the main accent. Use gold to highlight an important value, chart series, or part of a diagram. Pair gold fills with navy text for contrast.
- Use Poppins for all slide text, including titles, body text, captions, footnotes, tables, and chart and diagram labels. Set the font in both the theme and the individual elements that have their own font settings. Use weight and size to create a clear hierarchy.
- Keep margins and alignment consistent across the deck. Vary the layout according to what each slide needs to explain.

### Layout and figures

- Give each slide one main idea and a clear focal point. Use a large chart for a result, a labelled diagram for a process, a prominent figure for a key metric, or an aligned comparison for two options.
- Give the main figure room to read from a distance. Place short explanations and annotations next to the evidence they describe. Use white space to make the reading order clear.
- Use figures on content slides wherever they help the audience understand a relationship, scale, sequence, or comparison. Use relevant photos for context. Keep charts, tables, and diagrams editable when the format supports it.
- Label figures with the units, categories, and sources the reader needs. Distinguish project targets, plans, and observed results. Use direct labels or other cues so colour alone does not carry meaning.
- Skip stock taglines, repeated cards, decorative badges, and generic imagery. Use direct titles that name the topic or state a takeaway supported by the evidence.

### Compact decks

- When the user asks for a compact deck, use one slide per content section. Combine source slides that cover the same section into one slide with a clear title, a main figure where useful, and the points the audience needs during the presentation.
- Move supporting examples, extra numbers, and source details into speaker notes when the format supports them. Retain essential caveats and context on the slide. Respect any explicit total slide count, including the cover if one is included.

### Final review

- Open the exported deck and review each slide at presentation size. Check that Poppins is used throughout, the theme is consistent, and text, labels, and figures fit without clipping or overlap.
- Check that figures match the underlying evidence and that a compact deck has exactly one slide per content section.
